# Manual Review Cases

While building the segments, I noticed some customers that don't fit cleanly into any group. These are the ones where I'd want to take a second look before deciding what to do. Here are 12 of them.

---

## Case 1: CUST00106 — A Champion we lost

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Recency | 61 days |
| Frequency | 7 orders |
| Monetary | ₹4,352 |
| Tickets | 1 |
| Churned? | Yes |

This one surprised me. 7 orders, over ₹4,000 spent, scored high enough to be a Champion. And then they left. Looking at their profile, they had 1 support ticket. Maybe that experience was bad enough to push them away, or maybe there was something else going on that the data doesn't show.

If I could go back in time, I'd want someone from the team to call them right after that ticket was closed. When your best customers have even one bad experience, it's worth a personal follow-up.

---

## Case 2: CUST00112 — Champion with returns, still buying

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Recency | 0 days |
| Frequency | 8 orders |
| Monetary | ₹5,576 |
| Return rate | 12.5% |
| Tickets | 1 |
| Churned? | No |

This person has a ticket AND returns, but they literally ordered on the snapshot day and are still one of our best customers. Not every complaint means someone is about to leave. Some customers just have high expectations and they'll tell you when things aren't perfect, but they keep coming back.

I'd leave them in Champions and not overreact. The returns might just be product fit issues like wrong shade or wrong size, not actual dissatisfaction.

## Case 12: What I learned from these reviews

Looking at all 12 cases, I noticed a few patterns:

1. **Support tickets are a weak signal on their own.** Some customers with tickets churn (Case 1), others don't (Cases 7, 8). The ticket content matters more than the count.

2. **Web activity is an underused signal.** Cases 3, 4, 9, and 10 all had high web sessions. Browsing without buying is a strong signal of intent, and we're not acting on it.

3. **The scoring system has blind spots.** Cases 9 and 11 show that percentile scoring can misclassify customers who are "good" in absolute terms. A customer spending ₹4,201 shouldn't be called Dormant even if others spend more.

4. **One bad experience can erase years of loyalty.** Case 1 (CUST00106) is the perfect example. 7 orders, ₹4,352 spent, and one support ticket later they're gone.

These edge cases are exactly why we need the churn prediction model in Part 3. RFM segments are a great starting point, but they can't capture the full story.
